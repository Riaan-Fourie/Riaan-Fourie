### Hi, I'm Riaan 👋

For years I built digital twins of physical plants, including one for a UK wastewater treatment works. Every time, the same lesson landed. The model is never the hard part. Getting it to hold up against real data and real operators is. The technology rarely made or broke a project. What did was how tightly it was wired into the people and the process around it.

I build AI the same way now. The model anyone can buy is the easy part. The real work is wiring it into your data, into the way your team actually operates, and into someone inside who can tell when it's wrong. Get that right and the routine, same-every-time work shifts to the system, while the judgment stays with your people. The AI does the doing, so they stay free to do the deciding.

And the line keeps moving. As the system takes on the settled, repeatable work, the people climb with it, toward the calls a machine can't make: reading a specific customer, a market that just shifted, what's true this week and wasn't last. The AI only knows what has already been done. A person knows what needs doing next. I've seen it on our own team, where someone with no software background grew into harder and harder decisions as the routine fell away beneath them.

Three of us build it directly, inside the Wilsch group (around 80 people, German IT since 1959, with its own data centres). That backing lets us take a system the whole way down, from the AI to the hardware it runs on.

**At Wilsch AI, we build:**
- **AI-in-a-Box**: one clear job, run on your own hardware.
- **Fractional AI Department**: one part of your business at a time handed to AI, while you stay in charge.
